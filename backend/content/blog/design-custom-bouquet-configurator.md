## Your bouquet, minus the guesswork

Every week someone messages me: *"I want blush roses but my bridesmaids are in dusty blue and I don't know how many stems I need."* That conversation used to take four emails. Now it takes five minutes in the **Custom Bouquet Configurator** — my online builder that turns your colours and budget into a made-to-order paper bouquet.

Here is how it works, what to think about before you start, and what happens after you hit order.

## Step 1 — Pick your blooms

Start by choosing the flower mix: roses for classic, peonies for soft and full, natives for a structural Australian look, or a custom mix. Each bloom type has its own stem price, so the builder can price as you go.

Not sure yet? Hold a real one first — the [Single Stem Peony](/product/paper-peony-ivory-single) gives you the exact paper, weight and finish I use in bouquets, posted for the cost of a coffee.

## Step 2 — Colour and paper

- **Colour:** pick from the palette swatches or type a custom shade — I match dress colours, corporate branding, even a hex code from an invite.
- **Paper:** textured (my default — light plays across it like a real petal) or smooth (cleaner, more graphic).
- **Weight:** 65lb for a soft, lifelike drape; heavier stock if the bouquet will travel hard.

A little colour theory: three related tones — one light, one mid, one deep — always photographs better than a flat single colour. Blush with ivory and a whisper of sage is the combination Perth weddings keep choosing, and it is a great default if you are unsure.

## Step 3 — Stems and greenery

The builder shows **live pricing** as you drag the stem count. Rough guides I share with clients:

- **Bridesmaid:** 7–9 stems, minimal greenery
- **Bridal:** 12–15 stems, moderate greenery, slightly fuller head
- **Statement bridal:** 18+ stems, often with trailing greenery

Greenery changes the silhouette more than people expect. Eucalyptus softens and widens; wattle adds native texture; a single trail of vine makes the whole thing look garden-gathered. It is the cheapest upgrade in the builder.

## Step 4 — Review and order

Everything you have chosen lands on one summary: spec, price, lead time. Send it, and it enters my studio queue as a custom art order. You will get:

1. **A proof** — the finished spec plus a colour reference, before I cut anything.
2. **Making** — cutting, curling and assembly, typically **2–3 weeks** for a full bouquet (rush options exist — just ask).
3. **Shipping** — boxed upright, Australia-wide, free over $150.

Prefer to design with me in person? The same spec gets built at the bench in my Perth studio over a cuppa — book it alongside a workshop ticket.

## What the configurator cannot do (yet)

Be upfront: custom dimensions like stem height and a full pattern-matched colour scan are still done by email — the builder handles colour, composition and count. Anything unusual (a bouquet shaped around a family heirloom brooch, a corporate colour no swatch covers), send me a note and we will spec it the old way.
