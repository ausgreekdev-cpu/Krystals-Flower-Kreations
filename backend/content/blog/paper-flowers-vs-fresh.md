## I am a paper florist — so let me be fair

I make paper flowers for a living, and I still buy fresh blooms for my own kitchen table. Paper and fresh are not competitors; they solve different problems. If you are deciding for a wedding, a gift or a room, here is the honest version — including the cases where fresh wins outright.

## What paper does better

- **It lasts.** Not weeks — years, with a [light care routine](/blog/care-guide-paper-flowers). A gift stays a gift.
- **No allergies.** Hayfever sufferers can hold a paper bouquet to their face without consequence. This alone decides it for a lot of Perth families in spring.
- **Out-of-season colour.** Want blush garden roses in 40°C January? Paper does not care what is in season.
- **Precise colours.** Bridesmaids' dresses matched to the petal, not the closest bunch at the market.
- **Travel-proof.** Paper goes in a car, a plane cabin or a storage box and arrives intact. Fresh flowers travel like they are being evicted.
- **Reusable and mix-and-match.** Centrepieces live on after the event; stems can be re-arranged into a different bouquet later.
- **No water logistics.** Nothing to refill, nothing to leak onto a hire tablecloth.

## Where fresh flowers genuinely win

- **Scent.** Paper can be scented lightly, but nothing equals a real ranunculus. If fragrance is the gift, buy fresh.
- **The gesture of immediacy.** A same-day bunch from a florist says "I thought of you today". Paper says "I thought of you for longer" — different, not identical.
- **Living, changing material.** Some people love watching a bloom open. Paper is finished the moment it is made.
- **True perishability as aesthetic.** There is a beauty in flowers that are temporary. Paper cannot do wabi-sabi.
- **Per-kilo budget for huge installs.** For a one-night event with a small budget and no keep-sake plan, bulk fresh stems can be cheaper per table — though usually not once delivery and setup labour are counted.

## The cost picture

Fresh per-table centrepieces in Perth: commonly $80–$150+, one-time. Paper equivalents: higher up front (my [Table Centre](/product/native-bundle-table-centre) is built to keep), then **$0 forever**. For a wedding where pieces become home décor, paper's effective cost per year of use approaches zero. For a dinner party next Saturday, fresh is simpler and I will say so.

## The hybrid I recommend

Most of my favourite work mixes both:

- **Paper bridal bouquet, fresh ceremony arch** — you carry yours forever, the arch stays with the venue.
- **Paper buttonholes and bouquets, fresh table runners.** Small paper pieces survive the whole day without water; fresh stays cheap where it is meant to be temporary.
- **Paper flowers for the allergies row, fresh everywhere else.**

## So, which one?

Choose **paper** if it needs to last, travel, match exactly, or be held by someone who cannot handle pollen. Choose **fresh** if scent, immediacy and living material are the whole point. Choose **both** for anything with a ceremony and a reception.

And if fresh is the answer — genuinely, happily, go and buy the freshest bunch you can find. Both end with flowers in a room, which was always the point.
