## The good news first

Paper flowers do not need water, do not drop petals and do not die — but they do need a little housekeeping. Treated well, every piece in my studio still looks right **years** after it was made. Treated badly, paper has two enemies only: dust and damp. Everything below comes back to those two words.

## Dust them properly (and lightly)

Dust abrades paper's surface colour, especially on dark plum and navy petals.

- **Once a month:** a soft clean makeup brush or a cool-blowing hairdryer on its lowest setting, held at distance. Never a damp cloth, never a wet wipe.
- **Feathered and textured blooms** (wattle, banksia): short puffs of air only — brushing flattens the fibre details you paid for.
- **Do not vacuum directly.** If you must, cover the nozzle with stocking first.

## Sunlight: the slow fade

Pigments in coloured cardstock fade under sustained UV, same as anything. Perth light is strong — this matters more here than almost anywhere else.

- **Ideal spot:** indirect daylight, out of the afternoon sun's path.
- **Acceptable:** a north-facing room where the bouquet does not sit in direct window light all day.
- **Avoid:** windowsills, glass-topped tables under skylights, and anywhere the piece gets a daily beam of sun.

If the room is bright, rotate the arrangement a quarter-turn each season so any fade is even rather than one-sided.

## Humidity and water — the two real risks

- **Never near water.** Bathroom vanities, kitchen splash zones, uncovered vases: no. Water spots on paper are permanent.
- **Humidity above ~70%** for long periods can soften glue joints and curl edges. Our Perth winters get there. Keep blooms in living areas with normal airflow; if a room feels clammy, a dehumidifier run weekly fixes it.
- **No real-water vase ever.** If you want the vase look, fill the vessel with dry sand, floral foam beads or decorative stones.

## Fixing a bent petal

Petals recover — do not bin the bloom:

1. Support the petal from behind with a fingertip.
2. Warm it gently with a hairdryer on low (heat makes paper pliable again).
3. Hold the curve you want for ten seconds as it cools.
4. A creased edge can be pressed flat under a book with baking paper either side.

Wired stems — like the [Blush Rose Bouquet](/product/eucalyptus-paper-rose-bouquet-blush) — are re-posable forever: bend the stem back, re-pose the head, done. That is the armature doing its job.

## Storage and travel

- **Transport upright** in a box, blooms not touching the sides. Never flat in a car seat.
- **Dust before boxing**, not after — dust ground into storage is set dust.
- **Long-term storage:** a lidded tub with the lid not sealed airtight, in a climate-controlled room. Add a silica sachet, not newspaper (ink transfers).
- **Wedding pieces after the day:** pop them back in their box the same night, before the bouquet gets dropped behind a couch.

## A yearly five-minute routine

Once a year: dust, one sun check, one gentle squeeze test on glue joints (loose petal? a dot of craft glue, thirty seconds). That is the entire maintenance schedule for a [Table Centre](/product/native-bundle-table-centre), a [Single Stem Peony](/product/paper-peony-ivory-single) or a full bridal bouquet.

Enjoy them. They were built to be looked at, not looked after.
