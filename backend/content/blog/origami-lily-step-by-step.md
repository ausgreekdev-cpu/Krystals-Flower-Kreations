## Before you start

You need one square of paper — 15cm is ideal for a first attempt, 7.5cm once you are confident. Thinner paper holds more creases without bulk: kami origami paper is perfect, though printer paper cut square works. Have a bone folder or a fingernail ready; sharp creases are the difference between a lily and a lumpy thing.

Work on a flat surface, and make every crease deliberate. The lily is built from a bird base, so if you have folded a crane, two steps below will feel familiar.

## The 12 steps

### 1. Start white-side up, fold in half to a triangle

Bring one corner to the opposite corner. Crease firmly across the long edge, then unfold.

### 2. Fold in half the other way, then unfold

You now have a cross of creases across the square.

### 3. Collapse into a square base

Turn the paper over. Push the horizontal creases inward so the four corners come together and the paper collapses into a smaller square with the open end pointing toward you. This is the **square base** (preliminary base) — the lily and the crane both live here.

### 4. Petal fold the right flap

Take the right-hand layer's outer edge and fold it to the centre line. Repeat with the left-hand layer of that same flap. You have made a kite shape.

### 5. Fold the top triangle down

Bring the small top triangle down over the two folds to lock a crease, then unfold everything from steps 4–5. You only wanted the crease lines.

### 6. Lift into a petal fold

Open the right flap slightly and pull its bottom point upward, flattening along the creases you just made. The kite reverses into an elongated diamond. This is a **petal fold**.

### 7. Flip and repeat on the back

Turn the model over and repeat steps 4–6 on the reverse flap.

### 8. Repeat on the two hidden flaps

You now have four flaps total. Fold the model like a book — bring one finished petal over to reveal an unfolded flap — and repeat steps 4–6. Do it again on the last flap. Every side should now be the same elongated diamond.

### 9. Narrow the petals

On the front face, fold the lower outer edges in to the centre line to slim the petal. Flip and repeat on all four faces. This is where most people stop folding too early — **the slimmer the base, the more open the flower**.

### 10. Fold down the four petals

Peel each of the four top points outward and downward. They should stand away from the centre like an open lily.

### 11. Curl the petal tips

Run each tip over a bone folder or the back of a scissor blade — thumb on top, paper against the edge, one confident pull. A real lily's tips recurve; straight tips are the tell of a rushed fold.

### 12. Inflate the base

Bring the narrow base to your lips and blow gently into the small hole at the bottom. The flower body puffs out. If it resists, gently squeeze the sides — the internal creases just need permission.

## The three creases beginners get backwards

- **Collapsing the wrong way.** The square base opens toward you, not away. If your petals end up on the outside, your open edge was flipped in step 3.
- **Skipping the narrowing in step 9.** Fat petals will not curl convincingly. Thin them.
- **Curling before inflating.** Inflate first, then curl — otherwise you crush the petals you just shaped.
