## The problem is the air, not your machine

If you cut here in Perth, you have seen it: a petal that should be glass-clean comes off the mat with fuzzy edges, tiny uncut tags holding on, or a fine drag line where the blade skipped. It is not a blunt blade and your machine is not broken. Our summer humidity soaks into cardstock, and damp paper cuts like damp cardboard.

I run a Maker and an Explore daily in a Fremantle-side studio ten metres from the water. Here is the settings routine that keeps my cuts clean through every humid front.

## Condition the paper before it hits the mat

- **Flatten sheets under a heavy book overnight** if they have been stored somewhere open — humidity makes paper curl, curling changes how the blade meets it.
- **Work with small batches.** Pull ten sheets from the pack, cut them, then seal the pack again. Left out, a pack reaches equilibrium with the room in under an hour.
- **Store cardstock with silica sachets** in the tub — $2 from the hardware store, saves entire colour runs.

## My humidity-proof settings

These are on a fine-point blade, standard green mat:

- **Canson 65lb / Colorplan 175:** custom material — pressure **more**, cut **two passes**. The second pass takes almost no force on dry paper but on damp paper it is the difference between "cut" and "almost".
- **Small petals under 3cm:** same settings, then **weld or nest shapes at least 5mm apart** — close nesting on humid paper lets one piece lift and drag its neighbour.
- **Fine details (filigree, veins):** drop to pressure **default**, speed down to 3, and mirror-cut. Slower means the blade recuts its own path instead of skipping along a damp fibre.
- **Blade depth:** do not compensate for humidity by pushing pressure through the roof. Clean the housing, wipe the blade on damp-free paper, and let two passes do the work.

## Mat tack rules in humid weather

A sticky mat grabs damp paper harder — pieces tear when you lift them:

- **Blot the mat** with a lint-free cloth before each batch; humidity leaves a film.
- **Weed straight off the mat** with a spatula, low angle, while the paper is still cool from cutting.
- **Re-tack with a brayer**, not your palm — palm oils plus humidity equals residue on your petal faces.

When the mat goes from tacky to grabby to dead within one session, it is the humidity in the glue, not the mat's age.

## When to just stop cutting

If sheets are visibly wavy the moment you lay them down, do not fight it — stack them, weight them, and cut after the front passes. I have thrown away more petals learning that lesson than any setting ever cost me. One hour of dry paper beats a whole mat of near-misses.
